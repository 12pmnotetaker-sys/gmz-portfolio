### nalp-financial-benchmark-2025  [T2 / medium]
CLAIM: The 2025 Financial Benchmark Report, compiled by Industry Insights
  from confidential surveys submitted by 142 organizations representing 344
  locations covering FY2024, reports that the typical company saw 8.5
  percent sales growth in 2024 versus 15.7 percent in 2022, a 13 percent
  median change in contract value per installation job and a median customer
  count up 13 percent to 355 clients per firm, and breaks gross profit out
  by work type including design build, with breakdowns by revenue size,
  profitability and region.
FIGURE: 142 organizations / 344 locations; 8.5% sales growth (typical company); +13% median contract value per installation job; 355 median clients
SOURCE: National Association of Landscape Professionals, Financial Benchmark Report announcement (data collected by Industry Insights)
URL: https://blog.landscapeprofessionals.org/gain-competitive-insights-with-the-2025-financial-benchmark-report/
PUBLISHED: 2025-10-14   SAMPLE: 142 organizations representing 344 locations, FY2024 data, confidential surveys   GEO: US national
BEARS ON: The only landscape industry benchmark located that discloses its
  sample and segments gross profit by design build versus maintenance. If an
  outside yardstick is wanted for the two service lines, this is the one
  worth buying into and participating in.
CAVEAT: Merges two duplicate entries describing the same report. 142 self
  selected firms nationally, skewed toward companies large enough to submit
  full financial statements, so not a probability sample; a four person
  Peninsula firm will land in a thin or absent cell. The design build gross
  profit figures are behind the paywall and unseen, so nothing about design
  build margins can be quoted from it. The 8.5 percent is quoted as the
  typical company, not explicitly a median. An assertion elsewhere that
  firms above $10M performed strongest is unsupported by the page.

### front-end-planning-alignment-payoff  [T2 / low]
CLAIM: Construction Industry Institute research summary RS213-1 reports that
  projects with better aligned teams during front end planning performed
  approximately 10 percent better on costs and over 16 percent better on
  schedules.
FIGURE: ~10% better cost; >16% better schedule
SOURCE: Construction Industry Institute, Front End Planning: Break the Rules, Pay the Price (RS213-1)
URL: https://www.construction-institute.org/front-end-planning-break-the-rules-pay-the-price
PUBLISHED: 2006-11-01   SAMPLE: 17 case studies from projects over $1.5B; sample behind the 10%/16% figures not disclosed   GEO: US national, capital projects
BEARS ON: The nearest evidence for a phased design agreement with a scope
  definition gate before construction is authorised: define scope to a
  standard, then commit. It is the reasoning behind charging for the
  definition phase separately.
CAVEAT: Twenty years old, with no sample size disclosed behind the two
  headline percentages. The population is industrial and capital projects
  worth over $1.5 billion. The mechanism may transfer to a residential yard;
  the magnitudes must not be quoted as though they do. A Project Definition
  Rating Index threshold attributed to this page could not be found on it
  and is recorded as a gap.

### reviews-widely-read-but-half-distrusted  [T2 / low]
CLAIM: Online reviews are widely consulted but only about half of readers
  believe them: 82 percent of US adults say they at least sometimes read
  online customer ratings or reviews before purchasing items for the first
  time, with 40 percent doing so always or almost always; among those who
  read online reviews, 51 percent say they generally give an accurate
  picture of product quality, while 48 percent say it is often hard to tell
  whether online reviews are truthful and unbiased.
FIGURE: 82% read at least sometimes; 40% always/almost always; among review readers, 51% accurate vs 48% hard to tell
SOURCE: Pew Research Center, Online reviews and ratings, in Online Shopping and E-Commerce
URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2016/12/19/online-reviews/
PUBLISHED: 2016-12 (fielded 2015-11/2015-12)   SAMPLE: 4,787 American Trends Panel respondents; margin of error ±1.94 percentage points   GEO: US national
BEARS ON: Explains why a contractor's own published content still matters in
  a review dominated market. The things a firm can publish that reviews
  cannot supply are the independently checkable ones: a licence number,
  named towns, a written process, references on request.
CAVEAT: Fielded in late 2015, so the data is over ten years old and the
  percentages should not be quoted as current. The 51 and 48 percent figures
  are shares of review readers, not of all adults. Covers first time
  purchases of products generally, not selection of a licensed contractor
  for a five figure construction project. Pew fits none of the tiers cleanly
  and is placed at T2 on the strength of a disclosed probability panel
  method.

### usher-burgett-consumers-value-extended-home-warranty  [T2 / low]
CLAIM: A survey of 162 US adults reported greater perceived value in
  extended new home warranties together with a stated willingness to pay for
  them, and a positive association with a builder provided preventative
  maintenance program.
FIGURE: n=162 US adults
SOURCE: Usher & Burgett, Consumer Perception and Willingness to Pay for Extended New Home Warranties, The Professional Constructor 44(1), Article 9
URL: https://open.clemson.edu/aic-journal/vol44/iss1/9/
PUBLISHED: 2019   SAMPLE: 162 US adults   GEO: US national
BEARS ON: The only tiered evidence located bearing on whether a warranty
  influences buyer behaviour. It points toward a warranty plus a paid
  maintenance plan being a saleable package rather than a giveaway.
CAVEAT: It does not establish that a warranty changes contractor choice,
  only that consumers say they value one. A dollar figure previously
  attached to this study was struck because the full text returned HTTP 403.
  The subject is new home warranties from production builders, not landscape
  remodel work; n=162 is small, recruitment unverified, and the study is
  seven years old. The journal is association refereed rather than an
  indexed scholarly journal.

### platform-margin-claims-unreliable  [T3 / low]
CLAIM: The vendor reports that manual tool users had no profit margins above
  15 percent, spreadsheet users clustered at 4 to 7 percent with only 9
  percent above 11 percent, while one in five of its own users reported
  margins above 11 percent, presented without any control for firm size,
  revenue or maturity.
FIGURE: spreadsheet users cluster 4-7%, 9% above 11%; vendor's users 20% above 11%; manual tool users none above 15%
SOURCE: Aspire 2026 Technology Trends Report (Thrive Analytics), reported in Landscape Management
URL: https://www.landscapemanagement.net/aspires-2026-technology-trends-report-why-integrated-platforms-are-key-to-landscaping-margins/
PUBLISHED: 2026-04   SAMPLE: more than 500 commercial landscaping professionals; field dates not disclosed   GEO: US national

  plus 15 crew licences), both plus an unquantified one time onboarding fee,

  least 60 days written notice to cancel, and publishes neither per user

FIGURE: Annual contract required; agreements auto renew; minimum 60 days cancellation notice; per user and implementation pricing unpublished

URL: https://arborgold.com/pricing/

  must be confirmed directly. Because per user and implementation costs are
  unpublished, total cost of ownership cannot be determined at all. Vendor
  self published.
