### bpc-7027-5-own-work-limit  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: B&P 7027.5(a) grants a C-27 licensee design authority only for work
  'to be performed and supervised by that contractor', so the statutory text
  ties the design right to the contractor's own anticipated construction
  rather than to design as a freestanding service.
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | (a) A landscape contractor working within the classification for which
  | the license is issued may design systems or facilities for work to be
  | performed and supervised by that contractor.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7027.5, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7027.5
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2012, Ch. 537, Sec. 1 (AB 1750), effective 2013-01-01; verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: GMZ's paid design-only agreement: whether a design phase sold
  under its own contract, where the client might not proceed to construction
  or might take the plans elsewhere, sits inside the C-27 design authority.
  Counsel must construe 'to be performed and supervised by that contractor'.
CAVEAT: The statute nowhere addresses what happens if the designed work is
  never built or is built by someone else; no case, AG opinion, or CSLB
  guidance resolving that was found. Subdivision (e) further limits the
  grant, verbatim per leginfo (verified 2026-08-15): 'Nothing in this
  section authorizes a landscape contractor to engage in or perform
  activities that require a license pursuant to the Professional Engineers
  Act (Chapter 7 (commencing with Section 6700)).'

### bpc-5641-4-la-act-exemption  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: B&P 5641.4 exempts a licensed landscape contractor from the Landscape
  Architects Practice Act for design of work the contractor will perform and
  supervise, but forbids use of the title 'landscape architect'.
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | A landscape contractor licensed under the statutes of this state,
  | insofar as they work within the classification for which the license is
  | issued, may design systems and facilities for work to be performed and
  | supervised by that landscape contractor and is exempt from this chapter,
  | except that a landscape contractor shall not use the title "landscape
  | architect" unless they hold a license as required under this chapter.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 5641.4, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=5641.4
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2024, Ch. 482, Sec. 25 (SB 1452), effective 2025-01-01; verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: The same 'performed and supervised by that contractor' limit
  appears in both the Contractors' State License Law (7027.5) and the LA
  Practice Act (5641.4); counsel can read them in pari materia. Also a hard
  rule for GMZ marketing and the design agreement: never the words
  'landscape architect' or 'landscape architectural'.
CAVEAT: Recently amended (effective 2025); any older secondary summaries
  citing pre-SB 1452 wording are out of date.

### ccr-832-27-c27-definition-no-design-grant  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: The C-27 classification definition in 16 CCR 832.27 describes only
  construction-side activity (constructs, maintains, repairs, installs,
  subcontracts, prepares and grades land) and assigns no design activity to
  the contractor; the only occurrence of 'design' is the participle
  'designed' describing the improved areas, so a C-27's design authority
  rests entirely on B&P 7027.5 and 5641.4, not on the regulation.
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | A landscape contractor constructs, maintains, repairs, installs, or
  | subcontracts the development of landscape systems and facilities for
  | public and private gardens and other areas which are designed to
  | aesthetically, architecturally, horticulturally, or functionally improve
  | the grounds within or surrounding a structure or a tract or plot of
  | land. In connection therewith, a landscape contractor prepares and
  | grades plots and areas of land for the installation of any
  | architectural, horticultural and decorative treatment or arrangement.
SOURCE: 16 CCR § 832.27 as reproduced on CSLB's C-27 classification page (regulator publication)
URL: https://cslb.ca.gov/About_Us/Library/Licensing_Classifications/Licensing_Classifications_Detail.aspx?Class=C27
PUBLISHED: Regulation amendment filed 1988-06-01, operative 1988-07-01 (Register 88, No. 23) per Cornell LII reproduction; CSLB page itself undated, fetched 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: Scope description for GMZ's license classification; confirms
  maintenance ('maintains, repairs') is squarely in-classification for the
  recurring accounts line, and that the design agreement must lean on the
  statutes, not the regulation.
CAVEAT: Verified on CSLB's classification page 2026-08-15, which shows
  'Authority cited: Sections 7008 and 7059, Reference: Sections 7058 and
  7059 (Business and Professions Code)'. The canonical govt.westlaw CCR
  portal was not fetched; the Register history rests on the Cornell LII
  reproduction. Original claim wording ('the word design does not appear')
  was corrected in audit: 'designed' does appear, but only describing the
  areas, not as a contractor activity.

  preservation, development and enhancement, which is the field the 5641.4

URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=5615
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2006, Ch. 564, Sec. 9, effective 2007-01-01; verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: Baseline definition counsel needs to measure GMZ's design

### bpc-5641-unlicensed-designer-boundary  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: B&P 5641 lets any unlicensed person prepare conceptual design
  drawings and plant-selection plans for a single family dwelling, but
  requires that construction documents, details, specifications, and grading
  and drainage plans be prepared by 'a licensed professional as required by
  law'.
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | This chapter shall not be deemed to prohibit any person from preparing
  | drawings for the conceptual design and placement of tangible objects and
  | landscape features or plans, drawings, and specifications for the
  | selection, placement, or use of plants for a single family dwelling.
  | Construction documents, details, or specifications for the tangible
  | objects or landscape features, and alteration of site requiring grading
  | and drainage plans shall be prepared by a licensed professional as
  | required by law.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 5641, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=5641
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2004, Ch. 691, Sec. 12, effective 2005-01-01; verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: Draws the line between what unlicensed 'garden designers' on the
  Peninsula may sell (conceptual only, single family dwellings) and what
  GMZ's C-27 license adds (construction-level documents for its own work);
  useful both for the design agreement's deliverables list and for
  positioning against unlicensed competitors.
CAVEAT: 'A licensed professional as required by law' is not defined in the
  section; the LATC's Permitted Practices document reads the licensed-
  professional universe to include C-27 contractors via 5641.4 (see latc-
  permitted-practices-c27-construction-documents). The LATC document also
  confirms the parallel exemptions for property owners (5641.1),
  nurserypersons (5641.2), architects/engineers (5641.3), golf course
  architects (5641.5) and irrigation consultants (5641.6).

### bpc-7151-2-hic-definition-design-only-silence  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: B&P 7151.2 defines 'home improvement contract' as an agreement
  between a contractor and an owner or tenant 'for the performance of a home
  improvement ... and includes all labor, services, and materials to be
  furnished and performed thereunder', without expressly including or
  excluding a design-only agreement.
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | "Home improvement contract" means an agreement, whether oral or written,
  | or contained in one or more documents, between a contractor and an owner
  | or between a contractor and a tenant, regardless of the number of
  | residence or dwelling units contained in the building in which the
  | tenant resides, if the work is to be performed in, to, or upon the
  | residence or dwelling unit of the tenant, for the performance of a home
  | improvement as defined in Section 7151, and includes all labor,
  | services, and materials to be furnished and performed thereunder.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7151.2, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7151.2
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 1991, Ch. 1160, Sec. 42; verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: Whether GMZ's standalone paid design agreement must carry the full
  7159 home-improvement-contract apparatus (statutory notices, cancellation
  rights, 7159.5 deposit cap). The text is silent on design-only; the answer
  turns on the definitional chain into 7151 and Civil Code 1689.5.
CAVEAT: The section's second sentence extends the definition to salesperson
  agreements 'for the sale, installation, or furnishing of home improvement
  goods or services', which pulls in the 7151/1689.5 chain quoted in the
  next finding. No statute, regulation, or published guidance expressly
  classifying a contractor's design-only agreement was found.

### bpc-7151-services-chain-civ-1689-5  [T1 / medium]
CLAIM: B&P 7151 makes 'the furnishing of home improvement services' itself a
  'home improvement', and defines those services through Civil Code 1689.5
  as services 'bought in connection with the improvement of real property',
  a chain broad enough that counsel could place a paid design agreement
  inside the home-improvement-contract regime.
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | "Home improvement" shall also mean the installation of home improvement
  | goods or the furnishing of home improvement services. For purposes of
  | this chapter, "home improvement goods or services" means goods and
  | services, as defined in Section 1689.5 of the Civil Code, which are
  | bought in connection with the improvement of real property. [CIV
  | 1689.5(d):] "Services" means work, labor and services, including, but
  | not limited to, services furnished in connection with the repair,
  | restoration, alteration, or improvement of residential premises, or
  | services furnished in connection with the sale or repair of goods
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7151 and Civil Code § 1689.5, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7151
PUBLISHED: BPC 7151 last amended Stats. 2021, Ch. 249, Sec. 1 (SB 757), effective 2022-01-01; CIV 1689.5 last amended Stats. 2020, Ch. 158, Sec. 4 (AB 2471), effective 2021-01-01; both verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: The safest-practice conclusion for GMZ: because 'services bought
  in connection with the improvement of real property' plausibly reaches a
  design fee, drafting the design agreement to comply with 7159 (and
  honoring the three-day cancel right) costs little and forecloses the
  argument. Counsel should confirm.
CAVEAT: 7151 also lists 'landscaping' and 'fences' expressly among home
  improvements (verified). CIV 1689.5(d)'s exclusions cover attorneys, real
  estate professionals, securities dealers, physicians, optometrists and
  dentists, not design professionals. CIV 1689.5(a) sets the home
  solicitation threshold at $25 or more 'made at other than appropriate
  trade premises'; 1689.5(f) defines senior citizen as 'an individual who is
  65 years of age or older'. The Civil Code section was fetched at https://l
  eginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sec
  tionNum=1689.5. The 'could place a design agreement inside the regime'
  step is the researcher's inference, not stated in any source, hence medium
  confidence.

### bpc-7159-e6-cancel-trigger-and-notice  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: Under B&P 7159(e)(6), the Three-Day (or Five-Day, for seniors) Right
  to Cancel notice must be included in every home improvement contract
  unless the contract was negotiated at the contractor's place of business,
  is subject to the Seven-Day Right to Cancel, or is an alarm company
  contract; the notice's operative text starts the clock when the buyer
  receives a signed and dated copy of the contract containing the notice.
FIGURE: 3 business days; 5 for buyers 65 or older
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | (A) The notice ... entitled "Three-Day Right to Cancel," or entitled
  | "Five-Day Right to Cancel" for contracts with a senior citizen, shall be
  | provided to the buyer unless the contract is: (i) Negotiated at the
  | contractor's place of business. (ii) Subject to the "Seven-Day Right to
  | Cancel," as set forth in paragraph (7). (iii) Subject to licensure under
  | the Alarm Company Act... [B](i): "You, the buyer, have the right to
  | cancel this contract within three business days. You may cancel by
  | emailing, mailing, faxing, or delivering a written notice to the
  | contractor at the contractor's place of business by midnight of the
  | third business day after you received a signed and dated copy of the
  | contract that includes this notice." (ii): References to "three" and
  | "third" ... shall be changed to "five" and "fifth," respectively, for a
  | buyer who is a senior citizen.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7159(e)(6), leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7159
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2025, Ch. 585, Sec. 1.5 (SB 517), effective 2026-01-01; quoted provisions verified present in the current text 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: GMZ signs design and construction agreements at clients' homes on
  the Peninsula, so the notice is required in both; because clients skew
  65+, in Atherton, Hillsborough and Woodside especially, the Five-Day
  variant will often be the correct one, and the cancellation clock runs
  from delivery of the signed copy containing the notice.
CAVEAT: Audit correction: 7159 was amended by SB 517 effective 2026-01-01,
  which the original research missed (it recorded the date as unknown); the
  (e)(6)(A) trigger, (B)(i) notice text, (B)(ii) senior substitution, and
  (a)(2) service-and-repair carve-out were all re-verified in the current
  post-SB 517 text, but what else SB 517 changed in the section was not
  diffed. 7159 is long and was quoted by targeted extracts, not reproduced
  end to end; counsel should pull the full current section, including
  paragraph (7)'s Seven-Day Right to Cancel. 7159(a)(2), verified verbatim:
  the section 'does not apply to service and repair contracts that are
  subject to Section 7159.10, if the contract for the applicable services
  complies with Sections 7159.10 to 7159.14, inclusive.'

### cslb-cancel-framing-vs-statute  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: CSLB's consumer guidance frames the three-day right as arising
  'unless the contract is negotiated at the contractor's place of business,
  and/or the contract price is under $25', importing the $25 threshold from
  Civil Code 1689.5's home solicitation definition even though B&P
  7159(e)(6)(A) itself contains no dollar threshold.
FIGURE: $25 threshold (from CIV 1689.5(a), not from 7159)
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | Unless the contract is negotiated at the contractor's place of business,
  | and/or the contract price is under $25, the buyer qualifies for a three-
  | day right to cancel. ... Starting in 2021, seniors (those 65 and older)
  | will have five days to cancel the following transactions: home
  | solicitation contracts, home improvement contracts, Property Assessed
  | Clean Energy assessment contracts, service and repair contracts, and
  | seminar sales contracts (AB 2471).
SOURCE: CSLB, 'Home Improvement Contracts — Warnings and Exceptions' (regulator publication)
URL: https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/Warnings_And_Exceptions.aspx
PUBLISHED: unknown (page undated, confirmed in audit 2026-08-15; references AB 2471, so post-2020)   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: The reconciliation counsel was asked for: the statutory trigger in
  7159(e)(6)(A) is purely locational (negotiated at the contractor's place
  of business or not), while CSLB's framing blends in the Home Solicitation
  Sales Act's $25 floor; both sources are now quoted so the design
  agreement's notice block can be drafted to satisfy the stricter reading.
CAVEAT: Consumer-facing guidance, not law; where it diverges from 7159's
  text, the statute controls. The page also states, verbatim: 'The right to
  a three-day notice automatically is canceled the moment the contract is
  signed and the contractor begins working on a "Service and Repair"
  contract.' DCA Legal Guide K-10
  (dca.ca.gov/publications/legal_guides/k_10.pdf) surfaced in search as a
  further T1 reconciliation source but was not fetched. Guidance page not
  yet updated against the SB 517 (2026) amendment of 7159, so treat its
  framing as potentially stale.

### bpc-7159-10-service-repair-conditions  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: A 'service and repair contract' escaping full 7159 formalities must
  satisfy all four of B&P 7159.10(a)(1)'s conditions: price $750 or less,
  buyer-initiated contact, no selling beyond what the particular problem
  reasonably requires, and no payment due or accepted until the work is
  completed.
FIGURE: $750 or less; payment only on completion
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | (A) The contract amount is seven hundred fifty dollars ($750) or less.
  | (B) The prospective buyer initiated contact with the contractor to
  | request the work. (C) The contractor does not sell the buyer goods or
  | services beyond those reasonably necessary to take care of the
  | particular problem that caused the buyer to contact the contractor. (D)
  | No payment is due, or accepted by the contractor, until the work is
  | completed.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7159.10, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7159.10
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2020, Ch. 158, Sec. 3 (AB 2471), effective 2021-01-01; verified current 2026-08-15   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: New for the maintenance line beyond Study 001: small ad-hoc
  repairs on GMZ's recurring accounts can use the service-and-repair form
  only if the client called first, GMZ does not upsell beyond the presenting
  problem, and GMZ collects nothing until done; CSLB's guidance page adds
  that the three-day notice right is canceled the moment the contract is
  signed and work begins on a service and repair contract.
CAVEAT: Condition (D) means the service-and-repair route is incompatible
  with any advance payment. Work exceeding $750 or contractor-solicited work
  falls back into full 7159 territory.

### bpc-7027-3-license-number-fraud  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: B&P 7027.3 punishes willful, intent-to-defraud use of a contractor's
  license number that does not correspond to one's own currently valid
  license with a fine up to $10,000 and/or up to one year's imprisonment,
  and applies to licensed and unlicensed persons alike.
FIGURE: Up to $10,000 fine; up to 1 year imprisonment
QUOTE (verbatim):
  | Any person, licensed or unlicensed, who willfully and intentionally
  | uses, with intent to defraud, a contractor's license number that does
  | not correspond to the number on a currently valid contractor's license
  | held by that person, is punishable by a fine not exceeding ten thousand
  | dollars ($10,000), or by imprisonment in state prison, or in county jail
  | for not more than one year, or by both that fine and imprisonment.
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7027.3, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (official)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7027.3
PUBLISHED: Last amended Stats. 2001, Ch. 728, Sec. 54, effective 2002-01-01 (audit corrected from the original's 2002-01-02)   SAMPLE:    GEO: California
BEARS ON: Closes Study 001's second open item: 7027.3 is a fraud statute
  about misusing a license number, not about classification scope; for GMZ
  it simply reinforces keeping the CSLB number accurate everywhere it
  appears (which the site's one-fact-one-home rule already enforces).
CAVEAT: Requires willfulness and intent to defraud; an innocent typo in a
  license number is not within the section's terms, though it invites other
  problems. Penalties are cumulative with other state law, and the registrar
  may cite unlicensed violators.
