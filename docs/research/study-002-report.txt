EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Three habits change immediately. First, the words "landscape architect,"
"landscape architecture" and "landscape architectural" never appear in GMZ's
marketing, proposals or design agreement: using them without that license is a
misdemeanor with a fine up to $5,000, while "landscape design by a licensed
landscape contractor" is safe. Second, every agreement signed at a client's
kitchen table, design or construction, carries the statutory right-to-cancel
notice, and because clients in Atherton, Hillsborough and Woodside often
qualify as seniors, the five day version will often be the correct one; the
clock runs from delivery of the signed copy containing the notice. Third, on
any rebate job, no lawn comes out and no materials are bought until the client
holds a written Notice to Proceed from their water supplier; starting early
forfeits the rebate permanently.

Study 001 established that a licensed landscape contractor may sign the MWELO
design plan but could not say how far the design authority reaches. Study 002
pulled the statutes verbatim. The authority comes from two parallel sections,
one in the contractors' law and one in the landscape architects' law, and both
tie it to work "to be performed and supervised by that contractor." The
regulator's own reference document then says in writing that a C-27's design
deliverables may include construction documents with specifications and
details. So GMZ's paid design package can be construction ready, not concept
only, which is exactly what separates it from what unlicensed garden designers
may legally sell.

What no source of any tier resolves, still, is the design-only case: what
happens when the client pays for design and never builds, or takes the plans
to another builder. No case, Attorney General opinion or licensing board
guidance addresses it. Whether the "performed and supervised" limit is judged
by what the parties anticipated at signing or by what actually happened
afterward is the single question GMZ most needs a California construction
attorney to answer, and the counsel brief assembles everything needed to ask
it in one visit.

Whether the standalone design agreement is itself a home improvement contract
is also nowhere stated. The definitional chain, home improvement contract to
home improvement services to services bought in connection with the
improvement of real property, plausibly reaches a design fee. The safe and
cheap answer is to draft the design agreement to comply with the home
improvement contract rules anyway: include the cancellation notice, honor the
cancel window, respect the deposit cap. Study 002 also added something new for
the maintenance line: small ad hoc repairs can use the lighter service-and-
repair contract form only when all four conditions hold, price $750 or less,
the client called first, no selling beyond the presenting problem, and no
money collected until the work is done.

Study 002 corrected the record as well as extending it. The largest
correction: the home improvement contract statute was amended effective
January 2026, which Study 001 missed; every provision GMZ relies on was re-
verified in the current text, but counsel should still review what else the
amendment changed. Smaller corrections came out of the audit: the C-27
regulation's wording, a Delaware fee bracket overstatement, and the famous "6
percent design fee," which turns out to be a narrow federal cap on producing
drawings for public works, not a price for design.

On the design fee itself, the honest position is now well documented. No
industry standard percentage exists in any source worth citing, and antitrust
history explains why no association publishes one. What does exist is a
consistent pattern in government fee schedules: the percentage rises as the
project shrinks. Washington pays 10.65 percent for full design services on
residence-class work at its smallest published size; Delaware pays up to 13
percent on small complex renovations; the Department of Defense just raised
its cap from 6 to 10 percent; California's own highway program accepts pre-
construction costs near 20 percent of construction. GMZ's 10 to 15 percent
sits inside the published public band, and the stronger client argument is the
one the federal government itself uses: qualified design is selected on
qualifications, and the research suggests avoided construction cost growth
dwarfs a few points of design fee.

The maintenance renewal escalator can now be drafted with real numbers. The
2027 minimum wage is certified at $17.40, a 2.99 percent step known before any
fall renewal notice goes out. The local all-items index is running 3.8 percent
while the gardening services index is running 12.3 percent, so the choice of
index is the pricing decision. The clause must name the exact index series and
a fallback, because the government skipped publishing a month during the 2025
shutdown, and it must reference an even month, because the local index prints
only six times a year. The annual renewal notice, legally required 15 to 45
days before renewal on auto-renewing accounts, should state the computed new
dollar amount rather than letting the formula run silently. Two executed
public contracts, Los Gatos and Fairfield, supply tested clause language and
one cautionary tale about naming an index that no longer exists.

Every permit, tree and rebate fact is strictly per town, and none transfers. A
crew working three towns in one week can face three different tree regimes,
three fence rules and three rebate programs on identical scopes. Study 002
captured verified rules for several towns, but many of the source pages are
undated, Palo Alto's tree thresholds rest on a nonprofit summary because the
city's own pages block automated retrieval, and a 2024 Palo Alto amendment of
unknown effect is outstanding. The practical product is a per-town intake
checklist, not a general rule.

On referrals, reviews and retention, the directions are solid and the
magnitudes are borrowed from other industries. Referred customers are more
valuable and more loyal, but the loyalty depends on the referrer staying a
client, so the right people to ask are established, satisfied maintenance
accounts, and keeping a referrer keeps two relationships. Retention effort has
several times the value leverage of acquisition effort, which favors the
recurring accounts. Review asks work best mentioning that other clients have
left reviews, and never with payment, which the platforms prohibit. The 30-day
and six-month call timing is practitioner convention; no study tests it.

What remains open, beyond the design-only question: local minimum wage
ordinances in GMZ's towns, which likely exceed the state floor; business
license rules in the nine towns besides Redwood City; MWELO status in every
jurisdiction except Menlo Park; and Cal Water's rebate rate, which is not
published anywhere public. Those are phone calls and account lookups, not
research problems, and several of the remaining questions can only be settled
by GMZ's own records.

THEMES


1. THE C-27 DESIGN AUTHORITY NOW STANDS ON QUOTED TEXT, AND IT REACHES CONSTRUCTION DOCUMENTS   [support: strong]

Both B&P 7027.5 and B&P 5641.4 grant design authority in identical terms
limited to work performed and supervised by that contractor, the C-27
regulation itself grants no design activity, the LATC's own reference states a
C-27's deliverables may include construction documents with specifications and
details, B&P 5641 confines unlicensed designers to conceptual work for single
family dwellings, and B&P 5640 makes unlicensed use of the protected titles a
misdemeanor. The frame is complete and verbatim; only the design-only edge
case remains unresolved.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Sell the design step as a construction-ready deliverable, which unlicensed
  competitors cannot legally offer. Recite in the design agreement that the
  plans are prepared for work GMZ will perform and supervise. Ban the
  protected titles from every page and proposal. Take the design-only
  question to counsel before leaning harder on the two-agreement structure.

  Supported by: bpc-7027-5-own-work-limit, bpc-5641-4-la-act-exemption, ccr-832-27-c27-definition-no-design-grant, bpc-5615-la-practice-definition, bpc-5640-misdemeanor-title-protection, bpc-5641-unlicensed-designer-boundary, latc-permitted-practices-c27-construction-documents

2. TREAT THE DESIGN AGREEMENT AS A HOME IMPROVEMENT CONTRACT UNTIL COUNSEL SAYS OTHERWISE   [support: moderate]

No statute or guidance says whether a contractor's design-only agreement is a
home improvement contract, but the definitional chain from 7151.2 through 7151
into Civil Code 1689.5 plausibly reaches a design fee. The cancellation-notice
trigger in 7159(e)(6) is locational, CSLB's guidance blends in a $25 threshold
from the Home Solicitation Sales Act, and the service-and-repair carve-out for
small jobs has four strict conditions including no payment until completion.
Advertising and license-number statutes close Study 001's two open items.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Drafting the design agreement to full 7159 compliance costs little and
  forecloses the classification argument. Include the right-to-cancel notice
  in both agreements signed at the client's home, defaulting to the five-day
  version for clients 65 and over. Use the service-and-repair form on
  maintenance-account repairs only when all four conditions hold, and never
  collect in advance on one.

  Supported by: bpc-7151-2-hic-definition-design-only-silence, bpc-7151-services-chain-civ-1689-5, bpc-7159-e6-cancel-trigger-and-notice, cslb-cancel-framing-vs-statute, bpc-7159-10-service-repair-conditions, bpc-7027-1-advertising-classification, bpc-7027-3-license-number-fraud

3. THE FEE PERCENTAGE IS DEFENSIBLE BY GOVERNMENT CURVES, NOT BY AN INDUSTRY STANDARD   [support: strong]

No association publishes a design-fee benchmark, a fact consistent with the
AIA antitrust decrees, and no tiered source states a residential landscape
percentage. What exists instead is uniform: every published government fee
schedule rises as project size falls, Washington's residence-class schedule
starts at 10.65 percent at its smallest tabulated size, Delaware reaches 13
percent on small complex renovations, the folk 6 percent is a narrow federal
drawings-production cap that Congress just raised to 10 percent for defense
work, and Brooks Act policy selects design on qualifications with QBS research
showing large avoided cost growth.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Never claim an industry standard exists. Present the fee as GMZ's price
  for a defined scope, contextualized by public fee schedules that are buyer
  publications, not seller price-fixing. Remember the honest comparison: GMZ
  sells design only, roughly two thirds of a full-service fee, on
  renovation-type sites smaller than any published curve reaches, both of
  which push the defensible percentage up, not down.

  Supported by: far-6-percent-design-production-cap, dfars-10-percent-milcon-cap-2024, brooks-act-qbs-policy-text, wa-ofm-fee-curve-1m-macc, wa-ofm-phase-split-design-share, wa-ofm-remodel-premium-landscape-extra, delaware-dfm-small-bracket-renovation-fee, caltrans-pe-under-20-percent-target, ncdot-pe-ratio-bridges-roadways, pe-percentage-inverse-to-project-size, qbs-2009-cost-growth-vs-design-fee, aia-antitrust-decrees-no-published-benchmarks

4. THE MAINTENANCE ESCALATOR CAN BE DRAFTED NOW, WITH THE INDEX NAMED EXACTLY   [support: strong]

Every input is in hand: the certified 2027 wage step (+2.99 percent to
$17.40), the statutory escalator formula worth copying (lesser-of cap, defined
window, rounding, announcement date), current prints for the local all-items
index (3.8 percent) versus the trade index (12.3 percent), local wage
benchmarks, the automatic renewal law's 15-to-45-day notice and cancellation
duties applying to every account as it rolls over after July 2025, and two
executed public escalation clauses. The failure modes are documented too: a
skipped publication month and a contract naming an index that no longer
exists.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Peg to the SF metro all-items index by exact series ID, reference an even
  month, state a fallback for unpublished months, and make the adjustment
  upward-only with a floor as Los Gatos does. Because a plain cost-of-living
  escalator underruns this trade's cost curve, pair it with a floor or wage-
  step add-on. Send each year's computed dollar amount inside the required
  renewal notice window rather than applying the formula silently.

  Supported by: ca-min-wage-2027-certified, labor-code-1182-12c-formula, cpi-gardening-lawncare-latest, cpi-sf-all-items-latest, cpi-sf-bimonthly-schedule, cpi-publication-failure-risk, oews-sf-37-3011-may2025, oews-sf-37-1012-may2025, arl-renewal-reminder-window, arl-online-cancellation, arl-material-change-notice, arl-ab2863-applicability

5. TEN TOWNS, TEN RULEBOOKS: PERMITS, TREES AND REBATES ARE STRICTLY PER JURISDICTION   [support: moderate]

Verified town-level rules now exist for turf rebate rates and their Notice-to-
Proceed sequencing, Menlo Park's local water ordinance with its designer audit
duties, heritage tree definitions in Atherton and Menlo Park down to the inch,
grading and retaining wall triggers, fence permit lines, and Redwood City's
business license. But sources are frequently undated, Palo Alto rests on a
nonprofit summary, and none of it transfers between towns.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Build a per-town intake checklist: incorporation status of the address,
  water supplier, tree survey against that town's thresholds, permit
  triggers for walls, fences and grading. Put tree and permit contingencies
  into the design agreement, where an Atherton heritage tree can add a month
  and $750 before construction pricing. Sequence every rebate job so the
  pre-inspection happens with the lawn still in place.

  Supported by: lawn-be-gone-per-agency-rates, valley-water-landscape-rebate-terms, cal-water-lawn-to-garden-preapproval, menlo-park-local-welo-ch1244, atherton-heritage-tree-permit-process, menlo-park-heritage-tree-definition, palo-alto-protected-tree-thresholds, smc-unincorporated-grading-exemption-250cy, portola-valley-sdp-and-fence-permits, woodside-retaining-wall-permit-trigger, redwood-city-permit-exemptions-cbc1052, redwood-city-business-license-contractors

6. REFERRALS AND RETENTION: THE DIRECTION IS PROVEN, THE MAGNITUDES ARE BORROWED   [support: moderate]

Peer-reviewed work shows referred customers are worth more and stay longer,
that their loyalty collapses if their referrer leaves, that retention effort
has several times the value leverage of acquisition effort, that reviews
matter most for independent firms and unfamiliar buyers, and that norm-based
review asks measurably work. Every study is banking, restaurants, platforms or
e-commerce; nothing measures home services, and the follow-up call timing has
no evidence at all.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Ask for referrals selectively: established maintenance accounts at least
  six months in, satisfied and unlikely to leave. Treat a lapsing referrer
  as two at-risk relationships. Steer referred design/build clients toward
  the maintenance book, where the loyalty advantage actually lives. At the
  follow-up call, mention that other clients have left reviews; never pay
  for one. Quote none of the borrowed percentages to clients.

  Supported by: referred-customer-clv-premium, referred-margin-erodes-loyalty-persists, referral-loyalty-hinges-on-referrer, wom-elasticity-vs-paid, crv-clv-different-customers, referral-campaign-field-experiments, thank-you-call-experiment-1976, service-recovery-paradox-meta, yelp-half-star-sellouts, yelp-one-star-revenue, review-solicitation-experiment, retention-elasticity-clv-framework

Maintenance Renewal and Escalator Design
A drafting package for the annual price adjustment on the roughly 24 recurring
accounts: an upward-only adjustment pegged to the SF metro all-items index
named by exact series ID with an even-month reference and a published-index
fallback, paired with a floor or wage-step add-on because the local index
underruns this trade's costs. Delivery rides inside the legally required
15-to-45-day renewal notice, which states each year's computed dollar amount,
with a worked example and the Los Gatos and Fairfield clauses as tested
precedent.
  Open items:
   - Peninsula city minimum wage ordinances above the state floor were not
     researched; several GMZ towns may have their own, which would move
     the wage-step floor for crews working there.

     B&P 17602 is legally unsettled; the recommended annual dollar-amount
     notice sidesteps rather than resolves it.
   - The SF-Oakland-Hayward index excludes Santa Clara County, so Palo
     Alto and Los Altos accounts escalate on an index that does not
     technically cover them; no finer BLS index exists.
   - Exact add-on size, cap level, and rounding convention are business
     decisions, not research questions.
   - Year 2 of the worked example uses hypothetical CPI and wage-step
     values, since neither is published yet.
   - Treatment of any future commercial or HOA maintenance accounts, which
     sit outside the consumer automatic renewal law.
