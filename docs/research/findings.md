### bpc-7159-home-improvement-contract-contents

claim: California law requires a home improvement contract and any changes to it to be in writing and signed, and dictates its contents, including the contractor's name, business address and license number, approximate start and completion dates, a description of the project and significant materials and equipment, progress payments stated in dollars and cents referencing the work performed, a mechanics lien warning and a three day (five day for seniors) right to cancel notice.
figure: $500 aggregate contract price threshold
source: California Business and Professions Code § 7159 (California Legislative Information)
url: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=7159.&lawCode=BPC
tier: T1
published: 2026-01
sample: n/a
geography: California
confidence: high
bears_on: Start dates, completion dates and payment schedules are per job statutory contract terms, so a site wide lead time or payment schedule creates a promise the contract must then honour job by job. Also applies to the design agreement if its price exceeds $500.
caveat: Residential home improvement only, not commercial work. Amended by Stats. 2025 Ch. 585 Sec. 1.5 (SB 517) effective 1 January 2026, so any secondary summary written before 2026 may be out of date.
