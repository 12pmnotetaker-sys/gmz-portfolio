GMZ RESEARCH KNOWLEDGE BASE — EVIDENCE GAPS
Study 002, compiled 15 August 2026
========================================================================

  1. No Attorney General opinion, CSLB newsletter article, or published
     disciplinary decision addressing a design-only agreement by a C-27
     licensee was found; whether 7027.5/5641.4's 'work to be performed and
     supervised by that contractor' tolerates a paid design phase where
     the client never builds, or takes the plans to another builder, is
     unresolved in every primary and regulator text located. This is the
     single question GMZ most needs construction counsel to answer.
  2. B&P 7159 needs a complete pull by counsel: it was amended by Stats. 2025,
     Ch. 585 (SB 517), effective 2026-01-01, which the original research
     missed. The quoted (e)(6) trigger, notice text, senior five-day
     substitution and (a)(2) carve-out were re-verified in the current
     text, but the full scope of SB 517's changes was not diffed,
     paragraph (7)'s Seven-Day Right to Cancel was not read, and the
     statutory notice form's formatting requirements (type size, duplicate
     copies) were not extracted verbatim. CSLB's undated consumer guidance
     pages may predate the amendment.
  3. Civil Code 1689.6 and 1689.7 (cancellation mechanics and notice form for
     home solicitation contracts) were not fetched; DCA Legal Guide K-10
     (dca.ca.gov/publications/legal_guides/k_10.pdf) surfaced in search as
     the obvious next T1 pull for the cancellation reconciliation but was
     not fetched.
  4. 16 CCR 832.27 was read from CSLB's official classification page (re-
     verified in audit, including the Authority/Reference notes); the
     canonical govt.westlaw.com/calregs page was not fetched, so the
     Register history (filed 6-1-88, operative 7-1-88, Register 88, No.
     23) rests on the Cornell LII reproduction.
  5. B&P 5640.1, named in the task, does not appear to exist: leginfo returned
     no section text, and the Practice Act's exemption sections enumerate
     as 5640, 5641, 5641.1 through 5641.6, 5642 and 5644, consistent with
     the LATC Permitted Practices citations. The intended cite was
     probably 5640 or one of the 5641.x exemptions, all of which were
     fetched.
  6. LATC's FAQ page (latc.ca.gov/consumers/faq.shtml) was never fetched; an
     earlier assertion that it repeats the C-27 design rule was removed in
     audit. Fetch it before citing it.
  7. The LATC 'Permitted Practices in California' PDF is undated on its face
     (September 2023 per XMP metadata) and disclaims legal effect; no
     dated LATC enforcement action or meeting decision specifically
     construing the 5641.4 contractor exemption was located (committee
     minutes surfaced in search but were not fetched).
  8. B&P 7027.1(b)'s full advertising-definition text was returned partially
     paraphrased by the verification fetch; the finding's quote was
     trimmed to verified phrases. Pull the full (b) text if the exact
     definition matters for the website review.
  9. No T1 or T2 source addresses pricing of a contractor's design phase, and
     no tiered source states a design-fee percentage for residential
     landscape work specifically: every residential percentage found
     (5-20%, 10-20%, 6-15%) lives on rejected lead-generation or vendor
     sites with no disclosed method, confirming Study 001's folklore
     finding at a second attempt. The statutes read are silent on design
     fees, so GMZ's pricing model is a business choice, not a compliance
     question, on the texts located.
 10. ASLA publishes no fee benchmark or fee survey with disclosed methodology
     that could be located; a trade-press claim about 'the latest ASLA
     business survey' could not be traced to any ASLA document with
     disclosed method and was discarded. The antitrust finding claims only
     consistency with, not causation of, this absence.

     (paloalto.gov PDFs and the amlegal-hosted municipal code both

     anything client-facing is written.
