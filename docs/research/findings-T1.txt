### bpc-7159-home-improvement-contract-contents  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: California law requires a home improvement contract and any changes
  to it to be in writing and signed, and dictates its contents, including
  the contractor's name, business address and license number, approximate
  start and completion dates, a description of the project and significant
  materials and equipment, progress payments stated in dollars and cents
  referencing the work performed, a mechanics lien warning and a three day
  (five day for seniors) right to cancel notice.
FIGURE: $500 aggregate contract price threshold
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7159 (California Legislative Information)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=7159.&lawCode=BPC
PUBLISHED: 2026-01   SAMPLE: n/a   GEO: California
BEARS ON: Start dates, completion dates and payment schedules are per job
  statutory contract terms, so a site wide lead time or payment schedule
  creates a promise the contract must then honour job by job. Also applies
  to the design agreement if its price exceeds $500.
CAVEAT: Residential home improvement only, not commercial work. Amended by
  Stats. 2025 Ch. 585 Sec. 1.5 (SB 517) effective 1 January 2026, so any
  secondary summary written before 2026 may be out of date.

### bpc-7159-5-down-payment-cap  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: B&P 7159.5(a)(3) provides that if a downpayment is charged on a home
  improvement contract it shall not exceed one thousand dollars ($1,000) or
  10 percent of the contract amount, whichever amount is less, and that
  thereafter the contractor shall neither request nor accept payment that
  exceeds the value of the work performed or material delivered.
FIGURE: $1,000 or 10% of contract amount, whichever is less
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7159.5 (California Legislative Information)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7159.5
PUBLISHED: 2024-01 (amended Stats. 2023, Ch. 403, Sec. 1 (SB 601))   SAMPLE: n/a   GEO: California
BEARS ON: If a paid design step is folded into the construction contract the
  cap bites and no meaningful design fee can be collected up front. This is
  the structural reason a two agreement model is used in California.
CAVEAT: Merges two duplicate entries for the same statute. The 12 point
  boldface requirement is NOT in this section; it is in B&P 7159. A separate
  exception exists for contractors who file a blanket performance and
  payment bond with the Registrar, so never more than 10 percent is not
  universally true. Whether a design only agreement is itself a home
  improvement contract is unresolved.

### bpc-7159-10-service-repair-750-conditions  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: A maintenance or repair visit qualifies as a service and repair
  contract, escaping the full home improvement contract regime, only if all
  four conditions hold: the contract amount is $750 or less, the buyer
  initiated contact to request the work, the contractor does not sell goods
  or services beyond those reasonably necessary to take care of the
  particular problem, and no payment is due or accepted until the work is
  completed.
FIGURE: $750 or less
SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7159.10 (California Legislative Information)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=7159.10.&lawCode=BPC
PUBLISHED: 2021-01 (amended Stats. 2020, Ch. 158, Sec. 3 (AB 2471))   SAMPLE: n/a   GEO: California
BEARS ON: Published copy promising that crews will spot opportunities and
  recommend upgrades on site, or any published prepayment terms, can defeat
  the buyer initiated and no advance payment conditions and pull routine
  visits into the full regime.
CAVEAT: The $750 ceiling is low against Peninsula labour costs, so many
  maintenance visits may already exceed it. Homeowners waive the three or
  five day cancellation right when signing a service and repair contract.
  Whether a given visit satisfies the four conditions is a legal judgement
  not made here.

### ccp-337-15-ten-year-latent-repose  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: No action may be brought to recover damages for a latent deficiency
  in the design or construction of an improvement to real property more than
  10 years after substantial completion, with latent deficiency defined as
  one not apparent by reasonable inspection, and the period running from
  substantial completion but no later than final public agency inspection,
  recordation of a notice of completion, use or occupation of the
  improvement, or one year after termination of work, whichever comes first.
FIGURE: 10 years
SOURCE: California Code of Civil Procedure § 337.15 (California Legislative Information)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=337.15.
PUBLISHED: unknown (retrieved 2026-08-15)   SAMPLE: n/a   GEO: California
BEARS ON: The long tail on buried work: drainage, subsurface irrigation,
  footings, retaining walls and waterproofing behind hardscape. The trigger
  events are documentary, which is a direct reason to date and record
  substantial completion on every job.
CAVEAT: Does not apply to actions based on willful misconduct or fraudulent
  concealment, which removes the ten year protection entirely. The outside
  limit does not shorten any applicable shorter period that starts on
  discovery.

  disciplinary action required within that same four or ten years or within

SOURCE: California Business and Professions Code § 7091 (California Legislative Information)
URL: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=7091
PUBLISHED: unknown (retrieved 2026-08-15)   SAMPLE: n/a   GEO: California

SOURCE: US Federal Trade Commission, Advertising FAQ's: A Guide for Small Business
URL: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business
PUBLISHED: 2001-04, edited 2025-01   SAMPLE: n/a   GEO: US national
BEARS ON: The regulatory version of the rule that undecided policy does not
  get published. An FAQ answer asserting a warranty term, a response time or
  a workmanship standard nobody has decided is an unsubstantiated objective
