GMZ RESEARCH KNOWLEDGE BASE
Study 001: Landscape design-build business process, systems and economics
Compiled 15 August 2026

========================================================================

WHAT THIS IS

A tiered-source review of how the landscape design-build trade operates, run
to test GMZ's current process against evidence rather than opinion. Every
finding carries its source, that source's tier, its date and its sample size.
Marketing pages, contractor blogs and any survey that would not disclose its
method were rejected and are not in here.

SOURCE TIERS

  T1 (peer-reviewed / university extension / government)
  T2 (association research, sample disclosed)
  T3 (trade or vendor survey, method disclosed)
 A T1 government statistic and a T3 vendor survey are not the same kind of
fact. Nothing in this base should move a decision on T3 evidence alone.

WHAT WAS FOUND

  71 verified findings: 50 T1, 4 T2, 17 T3
  75 evidence gaps, where no tiered source could be found
  7 themes
  3 tests of decisions already made

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The strongest evidence in this set is legal, and it is the part that should
change how the company operates first. California law, not marketing theory,
already decides most of what can be promised, published, signed and collected.
The down payment on a home improvement contract is capped at $1,000 or ten
percent of the price, whichever is less. The contract contents are dictated,
including approximate start and finish dates, signed change orders before the
covered work starts, and a three day cancellation notice, five days for
clients aged 65 and over. The license number has to appear in all advertising,
which includes the website. None of that is optional and none of it is a
matter of taste.

The second thing worth acting on is that the state's own water ordinance makes
a paid design step defensible. On new landscape of 500 square feet or more, or
a 2,500 square foot renovation, where a permit or plan check is involved, a
documentation package is required, and the design plan may be signed by a
licensed landscape contractor rather than a landscape architect. That is a
regulated deliverable the company can produce and sign itself. It is the
closest thing in this whole body of evidence to a reason a client has to pay
for design as its own step. Local cities may set their own thresholds and
nobody has checked Redwood City or San Mateo County.

Where the design question is genuinely unsettled is whether a design only
agreement, one the client could carry to another builder, is inside what a
landscape contractor's license allows, and whether that agreement is itself a
home improvement contract subject to the deposit cap. The statute grants
design authority for work the contractor will perform and supervise. No source
of any tier answers the design only case. That is a question for a California
construction attorney, and it is the single highest value unanswered question
here.

The third finding that should change behaviour is about closeout, not sales.
Two regulators, the state licensing board and the FTC, independently tell
homeowners to collect at least three bids, check recent similar work, verify
testimonials and get everything in writing. So every lead arrives inside a
comparison the buyer has been coached to run. And when things go wrong, the
licensing board's own numbers show that roughly half of the licensee
complaints closed before any field investigation closed for insufficient
evidence. Whoever holds the paperwork holds the advantage. That argues for
dated substantial completion records, signed releases from subcontractors and
suppliers, and photographs, more than it argues for any change to the website.

On money, the reliable numbers are about wages and prices, not about profit.
Field wages in this metro area run about 23 percent above the national median
for the same work, and a crew lead costs about 60 percent more per hour than a
field hand. Consumer prices for gardening and lawncare services rose about 4.8
percent a year from 2019 to 2023, while the local all items index rose closer
to 2 to 3.8 percent. A plain cost of living escalator in a maintenance
agreement has been a real price cut for this trade. The state minimum wage
rises every January on a published schedule, which argues for aligning
maintenance renewals with the calendar year.

Geography is the other reliable lever. The routing mathematics is solid and
old: doubling the number of stops inside the same service area cuts driving
per stop by about 29 percent, and travel out from the yard grows faster with
volume than travel between stops does. That is an argument for grouping stops
by day and for treating distance from Redwood City as a separate pricing input
from distance between clients. It is also why a single unqualified site visit
is the worst trip the company makes, since it gets no density benefit at all.

Now the honest part. Almost nothing here is about a four person residential
firm on the Peninsula. The census data confirms the shape of the business is
normal, since about 63 percent of California landscaping employers have fewer
than five people and 90 percent fewer than twenty, but every survey that
reports profit, software use or close rates samples commercial contractors
that are much larger, and most of those surveys are published by companies
selling the software being measured. There is no independent measurement
anywhere of whether buying a business management platform improves anything.
The one vendor comparison that claims it does compares its own customers to
non customers with no control for firm size, which is exactly the pattern
selection alone would produce.

Several things people repeat as facts are folklore and should be treated that
way. The typical design fee as a share of construction cost, the standard
consultation charge, the share of firms that credit design toward
construction, close rates by process, plant warranty norms, callback rates for
landscape work, and drive time as a share of the crew day, none of these could
be traced to any source that discloses how it was measured. In several cases
the widely quoted numbers trace to cost aggregator pages or software
marketing. They are not in this base and should not be added without a source
that says who was asked and how.

The practical consequence is that a few decisions have to be settled with the
company's own records rather than with research. Ninety days of logged leads
with source, stated budget, whether a visit happened, hours and miles used,
and outcome would settle the qualification question faster than any study. One
month of truck GPS would produce a better drive time number than anything
published. The company's own callback history over the last several years
would settle the warranty question. Those measurements are cheap, and there is
no published substitute for any of them.

THEMES


1. CALIFORNIA LAW ALREADY WRITES MOST OF THE RULES   [support: strong]

The strongest and most transferable evidence in the set is statutory and
regulatory, and it is specific to California residential work. Contract
contents, the deposit ceiling, the cancellation window, the license number in
advertising, and the narrow service and repair exception are all fixed by law
rather than by preference. Two independent limitation clocks run behind them,
four years on written contracts and ten years on latent defects, plus the
licensing board's own separate discipline window.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Set the deposit, cancellation notice, change order rule and license number
  once, in one place, and treat them as compliance objects rather than copy.
  Date substantial completion on every job, because the ten year latent
  clock starts from documentary events. Do not publish a site wide payment
  schedule or lead time, because those are per job contract terms.

  Supported by: bpc-7159-home-improvement-contract-contents, bpc-7159-5-down-payment-cap, ca-7159-required-boldface-notices, ca-three-day-right-to-cancel-kitchen-table, bpc-7030-5-license-number-in-all-advertising, bpc-7159-10-service-repair-750-conditions, ccp-337-1-patent-four-year-bar-excludes-owner-occupied-single-unit-residence, ccp-337-15-ten-year-latent-repose, bpc-7091-cslb-discipline-window-4-and-10-years, ca-automatic-renewal-law-ab2863, cslb-statutory-waiver-and-release-forms

2. THE PAID DESIGN STEP HAS A REGULATORY FOOTHOLD AND AN UNSETTLED LEGAL EDGE   [support: moderate]

The state water ordinance turns a permit ready plan set into a required
deliverable above 500 square feet of new landscape, and it lets a licensed
landscape contractor sign the design plan, which is what makes an in house
paid design step workable at four people. What no source resolves is whether a
design only agreement the client may take elsewhere sits inside a contractor's
licensing authority, or whether that agreement is itself a home improvement
contract subject to the deposit cap.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Sell design as the regulated, signed, permit ready deliverable, and carry
  the third party irrigation audit cost in the estimate because the designer
  and installer cannot perform it. Get counsel to answer the design only
  question before the two agreement structure is leaned on harder, and add a
  limited licence clause covering what a client may do with a paid plan set.

  Supported by: mwelo-500sqft-documentation-package-trigger, mwelo-contractor-may-sign-design-plan, mwelo-certificate-of-completion-six-elements, mwelo-auditor-must-not-be-the-designer-or-installer, ca-landscape-contractor-may-design-only-what-it-builds, ca-5641-unlicensed-conceptual-vs-construction-documents, bpc-7159-5-down-payment-cap, wsba-design-work-product-limited-license, front-end-planning-alignment-payoff

3. THE BUYER ARRIVES INSIDE A THREE BID COMPARISON, AND TRUSTS WHAT CAN BE CHECKED   [support: moderate]

Two regulators independently coach homeowners to collect several bids, compare
like for like, check recent similar projects and verify testimonials.
Separately, reviews are widely read but roughly half of readers doubt them,
and endorsement rules require testimonials to be honest, current and typical.
The things a firm can publish that reviews cannot supply are the checkable
ones: a licence number, named towns, a written process.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Publish how scope is defined and what the design step contains, since
  comparability is what the buyer has been told to look for. Store an
  approval date beside the approved flag on testimonials and re confirm at
  intervals, and publish a representative spread of projects rather than
  only the smoothest ones.

  Supported by: cslb-brochure-three-bids-references-similar-projects, ftc-tells-buyers-to-collect-multiple-estimates, ftc-endorsements-honest-opinion-and-continuing-approval, ftc-endorsements-must-be-typical-experience, ftc-substantiation-required-before-publication, reviews-widely-read-but-half-distrusted, asla-repeat-clients-win-work

4. DOCUMENTS DECIDE DISPUTES, AND THE DISPUTES WILL BE COSMETIC   [support: moderate]

The largest handover defect study found no structural defects at handover and
overwhelmingly surface, tolerance and functionality items, some surfacing only
after the client lives with the work. California's licensing board resolves
complaints at intake, where about half of the pre investigation closures were
for insufficient evidence, and most single item landscape disputes fall under
the mandatory arbitration ceiling. The state's own establishment definitions
supply a defensible one year anchor.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Build a closeout packet around the state's own certificate of completion
  list, add a scheduled 30 to 60 day return visit, and collect statutory
  releases from every subcontractor and supplier. Write the warranty term
  with exclusions before any warranty language reaches the website, since
  advertising a guarantee requires publishing its limits alongside it.

  Supported by: forcada-2016-defects-shift-from-structural-to-finish-at-handover, cslb-complaint-volume-and-intake-outcomes-fy2324, cslb-arbitration-thresholds-25k-and-50k, cslb-statutory-waiver-and-release-forms, civ-896-g12-landscaping-must-survive-one-year, mwelo-defines-establishment-period-as-one-year, uf-ifas-tree-establishment-scales-with-trunk-caliper, bpc-7159-home-improvement-contract-requires-no-warranty, ftc-warranty-guarantee-must-disclose-details-and-limits, cfr-239-3-satisfaction-guarantee-limits

5. RECURRING MAINTENANCE IS A PRICING AND GEOGRAPHY PROBLEM   [support: moderate]

Local wages, the trade's own price index and the routing mathematics all point
the same way. Field labour here runs well above national, a crew lead costs
about 60 percent more per hour than a field hand, gardening and lawncare
service prices have outrun the local cost of living index, and doubling stop
density in a fixed area cuts driving per stop by about 29 percent. Turnover,
not headcount growth, is the structural labour problem.

  What it means for GMZ:
  A plain cost of living escalator is a below market escalator for this
  trade, so it needs a floor or an add on. Group stops by day so each truck
  makes one round trip from the yard, price distance from the yard
  separately from distance between clients, and align renewals with the
  January minimum wage step.

  Supported by: oews-sf-metro-landscape-wages-may-2025, oews-bay-area-wage-premium-and-supervisor-rate, cpi-gardening-lawncare-escalation, bay-area-cpi-escalator-reality, ca-minimum-wage-trajectory, bhh-square-root-law-route-density, daganzo-vrp-linehaul-term, bls-ooh-grounds-maintenance-outlook, public-agency-maintenance-contract-structure, susb-receipts-per-employee-by-size, aspire-thrive-recurring-maintenance-revenue-share

6. ALMOST EVERY NUMBER ABOUT THE TRADE DESCRIBES A BIGGER FIRM   [support: strong]

Census data confirms a four to six person firm is the modal California
landscaping business, but the surveys that report profit, software use,
revenue mix and close rates sample commercial contractors with no small firm
breakout, and most are published by companies selling what is being measured.
The one benchmark that discloses its sample has 142 self selected firms
nationally, which leaves very few cells comparable to a small coastal
residential shop.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Treat any survey mean as describing the other half of the industry's money
  until it shows a size breakdown, and add roughly a fifth to a quarter to
  any national labour figure before it means anything locally. Use design
  build delivery research for its direction only, since it says schedule and
  accountability rather than lower price.

  Supported by: firm-size-distribution-us, susb-ca-landscaping-firm-size, nalp-financial-benchmark-2025, houzz-soi-2025-landscape-revenue, aspire-thrive-recurring-maintenance-revenue-share, end-to-end-platform-adoption, dbb-vs-db-fhwa-schedule-and-cost, revisiting-project-delivery-212-projects, db-margin-sizes-dbia-summary

7. SOFTWARE: HALF THE TRADE IS NOT ON A PLATFORM, AND NOBODY HAS MEASURED WHETHER IT HELPS   [support: weak]

The adoption numbers come from vendors, the pricing tiers that matter are
gated above the cheap entry prices, total cost of ownership cannot be computed
for any vendor from public information, and two apparently independent
shortlist names are now one company. The only outcome evidence is a vendor
comparing its own customers with non customers and controlling for nothing.

  What it means for GMZ:
  Staying off a platform is not an outlier position. If job costing is the
  reason to buy, price the tier that actually includes it and confirm the
  tier boundary in writing, budget for an unquantified onboarding fee, and
  treat an auto renewing annual contract with 60 day notice as the real
  switching cost. No vendor is documented as modelling design sold as a
  share of construction, which has to be checked by product demonstration.

  Supported by: end-to-end-platform-adoption, tool-sprawl, function-adoption-back-office-first, estimating-and-job-costing-gaps, platform-margin-claims-unreliable, lmn-granum-pricing, singleops-pricing, jobber-pricing-job-costing-tier, arborgold-contract-lock, aspire-no-published-pricing, vendor-consolidation-granum

DECISIONS TESTED AGAINST THE EVIDENCE


------------------------------------------------------------------------
DECISION: No written workmanship warranty. Nothing published, nothing signed,
the owner returns if something he installed fails.

VERDICT: EVIDENCE IS MIXED   (evidence strength: moderate)

PLAIN ANSWER:
Two of the three arguments go your way, and the third one does not. Nobody can
tell you what share of firms like yours put a term in writing, because that
number does not exist in any source worth citing. Nobody can show you that a
written warranty wins jobs either, so anyone promising it will lift your close
rate is guessing. Where you are wrong is the legal reasoning. Staying silent
does not shorten anything.

WHAT THE LAW ACTUALLY DOES:
California's home improvement contract statute never uses the words warranty
or guarantee, so what you offer is a business choice. But the contract itself
must be written and signed, so the four year written contract clock is what
runs, not the two year one for handshake deals. The four year cutoff for
obvious defects is expressly switched off for owner occupied single family
homes, which is most of your work. Buried work carries ten years. The
licensing board has its own four and ten year window that nothing you sign can
touch. So the unwritten position runs to the outer limits and defines nothing
inside them.

COST OF DOING NOTHING:
Every callback is negotiated from zero with a client you want to keep on the
maintenance book, because nothing excludes under watering, freeze, rodent
damage, third party alteration or lapsed maintenance. You cannot put a
warranty reserve into an estimate for an obligation you have never defined, so
callbacks come out of the current job's margin. And at the licensing board,
where about half the complaints closed before investigation closed for
insufficient evidence, these are decided on documents you do not have.

RECOMMENDATION:
Write a one year workmanship term with explicit exclusions, and a separate one
year plant establishment term with trees handled by trunk size or carved out.
Anchor the numbers to Civil Code 896(g)(7) and (g)(12) and the state water
ordinance establishment definition rather than trade folklore. Date
substantial completion on every job. Have California counsel review it, and
keep it off the website until the term, the exclusions and the duration all
exist together, because advertising a guarantee obliges you to publish its
limits.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS VERDICT:
Your own callback history. If the real rate of return and repair events over
the last several years is close to zero, the exposure and reserve arguments
shrink to noise and the answer becomes leave it verbal and document the
closeout instead. Also a tiered survey of small residential contractors
showing written terms are near universal here, which would harden the
recommendation, or one showing written terms raise claim frequency, which
would reverse it.

DECISION: No minimum project size and no starting price on the website.
Callers find out on the phone whether they are in range.

VERDICT: NO GOOD EVIDENCE EITHER WAY   (evidence strength: absent)

PLAIN ANSWER:
Nothing measures this. Not weak evidence, none. Every source that spoke to it
directly was a lead vendor or a marketing agency. Anyone quoting a percentage
for how much a published minimum cuts tire kickers is quoting a blog. Your own
numbers are the only way to settle it.

WHAT THE NEAREST EVIDENCE SAYS:
The best research on publishing prices at all comes from healthcare, and it
found the tools are mostly ignored, with small effects among the few who use
them. So a published number is a weak instrument either way. The more
interesting result is that under price transparency the well regarded
providers priced up rather than down. That route is closed to you right now,
because the site's projects, photos and testimonials are still placeholders. A
floor published above an empty portfolio is just a bare number.

COST OF DOING NOTHING:
Your buyers are told by both the licensing board and the FTC to collect at
least three bids and compare like for like. You supply nothing comparable, so
the comparison happens using the two firms that did say something, usually on
price. All the qualification work stays on your phone and your windshield as
an untracked tax. And the one route by which disclosure helps a good firm
stays shut while the site is stubs.

RECOMMENDATION:
Keep the no published minimum for now, because spare capacity makes an
unqualified visit nearly free and the market has been soft, but treat that as
right for a reason that expires. As the next two or three real projects land,
replace the silence with a coarse budget band on completed work plus a
published fixed price and deliverable list for the design step. Never publish
a company wide starting price, because price and dates are per job contract
terms, and because the FTC requires a documented basis before an objective
claim runs.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS VERDICT:
Ninety days logging every inbound lead: source, stated budget or refusal to
state one, whether a visit happened, hours and miles used, outcome. Under
about one in five out of range and short visits means drop the question. A
third or more of your windshield time means a coarse published band pays for
itself. Also note this flips when the book fills, so it needs a review date
rather than standing as settled policy.

DECISION: Free site visit, plus a quick concept and a planning budget at no
charge, bounded at about an hour of effort and scaled to property size.

VERDICT: EVIDENCE IS MIXED   (evidence strength: weak)

PLAIN ANSWER:
The free visit holds up. The free take away does not. How common it is to
charge for a consultation, and what charging does to lead quality, are both
unmeasured by any source worth citing, so nobody should tell you to start
charging at the door, including me.

WHY THE VISIT IS FINE:
The credence goods research says the party who defines the problem controls
the price, and that a homeowner who arrives with a confident wrong self
assessment gets quoted the most. Getting on site early and free is how you
become the one defining the scope. That is direction only, from taxi meters
and laptop repairs, so no number transfers.

WHY THE FREE CONCEPT AND BUDGET IS NOT:
A concept with a budget is a scope definition, and your own licensing board
tells homeowners to obtain three comparable bids. You are solving the
comparability problem for the buyer, for free, and handing the answer to two
competitors who never left their yard. When the trade drafts around design
work that was paid for, it attaches a limited licence restricting use to that
project. Your free version carries none of that. The unpriced budget number is
also an undocumented representation you will be held to.

ON SCALED TO PROPERTY SIZE:
Wrong scalar. Design effort scales with how complicated the program is, not
lot area. California hands you a better line: 500 square feet of new
landscape, or 2,500 renovated, with a permit or plan check, triggers the
regulated documentation package. Scaling free effort to property size puts
your biggest giveaways on exactly the jobs where the paid design step is most
defensible.

COST OF DOING NOTHING:
You pay an owner hour plus a full single stop round trip from the yard, the
worst travel to productive time ratio in the business with no route density
benefit at all, roughly three times per job won. On the two that do not close
you have also given a competitor your scope definition and your budget number.
Your free hour is your only design sales capacity, so every unqualified trip
is a paid design conversation you did not have.

RECOMMENDATION:
Keep the site visit free. Stop handing over the written concept and the
planning budget. End every visit either in a signed design agreement or in a
declined lead, and answer price curiosity with a coarse budget band on
completed projects rather than a job specific number produced in an hour.
Remember the three business day cancellation window, five days for clients 65
and over, sits in front of any kitchen table signature anyway.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS VERDICT:
Thirty logged visits with lead source, whether a concept and budget were
handed over, and whether a design agreement was signed. If cold web leads
convert close to referrals, the free concept is doing real work. A six month
test withholding the written concept while keeping the visit free would settle
it directly. And if the maintenance book already supplies most design work,
the question shrinks, because visits to known accounts are cheap and pre
qualified.
