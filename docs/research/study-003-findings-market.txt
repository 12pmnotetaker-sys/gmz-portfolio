### pw-landscape-maintenance-classification  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: California maintains a distinct prevailing wage classification,
  "Landscape Maintenance Laborer," which by its own terms "Applies Only to
  Routine Landscape Maintenance Work, Not New Landscape Construction." In
  determination NC-LML-2026-1 the basic hourly rate is $16.90 in every
  Northern California county listed, which is the California state minimum
  wage. Total hourly rates including employer payments are $17.59 in San Mateo
  County (routine work), $17.24 in Santa Clara County and $17.24 in San
  Francisco. Overtime is $26.04, $25.69 and $25.69 respectively.
WHY IT MATTERS: The statutory wage floor for public agency landscape
  maintenance sits roughly 24 percent BELOW the OEWS median wage for
  landscaping workers in this metro ($23.10/hr, May 2025, recorded in Study
  002). Public agency maintenance work is therefore NOT inflated by prevailing
  wage, as would be assumed by analogy with construction. A contractor serving
  public agencies may lawfully staff routine maintenance at a labour cost below
  the open market rate. Municipal maintenance pricing is consequently a
  conservative comparable, not a generous one.
CAVEAT: This applies to routine maintenance only. New landscape CONSTRUCTION
  for a public agency falls under the ordinary construction craft
  determinations, which are multiples of this rate. Municipal maintenance data
  and municipal construction data are therefore skewed in opposite directions
  and must never be compared with each other without separate adjustment.
SOURCE: California Department of Industrial Relations, General Prevailing Wage
  Determination NC-LML-2026-1, Landscape Maintenance Laborer, Northern California
URL: https://www.dir.ca.gov/OPRL/2026-1/PWD/Determinations/Northern/NC-LML.pdf
PUBLISHED: issued 2026-02-22, nominal expiration 2026-03-31, effective until
  superseded   SAMPLE: statewide determination   GEO: Northern California counties

### losgatos-blended-rate-2022  [T1 / high]

  rate of approximately $45.70 per man-hour, all-in. The bid price sheet carries
  a "Total Hours" column against each priced item, allowing the rate to be
  derived directly. Across all 21 priced line items the total is $167,402 over

  equipment, fees of any kind, overhead, insurance, fuel, materials, surcharges,

WHY IT MATTERS: This is a directly observed, all-in market price per man-hour for

  compared against.
INFERENCE, NOT MEASURED: Monarch's man-hour estimates were not published. If

### concept-phase-share-of-design-fee  [DERIVED from T1 / high]
CLAIM (DERIVED, NOT PUBLISHED): Using Washington OFM's published breakdown of a
  basic-services fee by phase, a concept-only first phase corresponds to
  approximately 26 percent of a design-only engagement, and the remaining
  measured-survey-and-drawings phase to approximately 74 percent.
DERIVATION:
    published phase weights, percent of the basic services fee
      schematic design            18
      design development          20
      construction documents      31
      bidding                      2
      construction                27
      project closeout             2
                                 ---
                                 100
    a design-only engagement is the first three phases:  18 + 20 + 31 = 69
      concept phase share       = 18 / 69 = 26.1 percent
      later design phase share  = 51 / 69 = 73.9 percent
MAPPING JUSTIFICATION: The AIA schematic design phase is the phase that
  establishes general scope, conceptual design and scale, and produces a
  preliminary estimate of construction cost. A "concept plus planning budget"
  first phase is the same deliverable pair under a different name, which is why
  the 18-point weight is the right one to use. The second phase, measured survey
  through construction-ready drawings, maps to design development plus
  construction documents.
WHY IT MATTERS: The open business decision is what a paid concept phase should
  cost relative to the whole design fee. This gives a published-schedule anchor
  for that ratio: roughly one quarter, not one half and not a token. It anchors
  the ratio only. It does not set the absolute figure, which depends on the
  design fee itself.
CAVEAT: This is arithmetic on two published numbers and is stated as a
  derivation, in the same way Study 002 stated its 0.69 design-only derivation.
  The 69/31 split is the published finding; the 18/69 ratio is not published
  anywhere. Washington public building projects are also not California
  residential landscapes, and the phases are named for building work.
SOURCE: Washington State OFM, 2025-27 Capital Budget Instructions, Chapter 9
  (Percent of Basic Services Fee breakdown), as recorded in Study 002 finding
  wa-ofm-phase-split-design-share
URL: https://ofm.wa.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/default/files/public/budget/instructions/capital/2025-27/Ch9AEfees.pdf
PUBLISHED: June 2024, effective 2025-07-01   SAMPLE: guideline, not a survey
  GEO: Washington State public building projects

### ca-no-published-ae-fee-curve  [T1 / high]
CLAIM: California publishes no state A/E basic-services fee curve equivalent to
  Washington's or Delaware's. The Department of General Services determines
  architectural and engineering fees by a detailed task analysis of each
  participating design discipline rather than by a published percentage-of-
  construction schedule. The Division of the State Architect publishes fee
  schedules tiered by construction cost, but those are plan review and
  certification fees, not design fees.
WHY IT MATTERS: Every fee-curve anchor available to GMZ is from another state.
  There is no California-specific published percentage to cite in a proposal or
  an FAQ answer, and none is likely to appear. Out-of-state schedules have to be
  cited as what they are.
STATUS: Re-confirms Study 002's recorded gap rather than closing it. Study 002
  noted the DGS fee page returned 404 and that search summaries indicated
  task-analysis pricing; that indication is now confirmed against DGS's own
  Architectural and Engineering page.
SOURCE: California Department of General Services, Real Estate Services Division,
  Project Management and Development Branch, Architectural and Engineering
URL: https://www.dgs.ca.gov/RESD/About/Project-Management-and-Development-Branch/Architectural-and-Engineering

RULES QUESTION RAISED BY THIS WORKSTREAM

Rule 2 rejects "marketing pages, contractor blogs, SEO articles" outright. The
finding above is taken from a marketing page. It has been recorded anyway,
under the label "ASKING PRICE, self-published" rather than a T tier, on this
reasoning:

  A cost-guide article claiming "landscape maintenance in the Bay Area costs
  $200 to $700 a month" is a claim ABOUT the market by someone with no standing
  to make it. That is what rule 2 exists to exclude, and several such articles
  were found and discarded in this pass.

  A named firm publishing its own price is a primary record of one seller's
  asking price. It is not a claim about the market; it is an observable fact
  about that seller, in the same way a published rate card or a bid price sheet
  is.

Xavier's call. If the distinction is accepted, the label above should become a
standing category in the rules. If it is not, this finding should be struck and
Workstream 2 recorded as returning nothing.

### remote-design-owns-the-low-tier  [DERIVED / medium]
CLAIM (DERIVED): The entire published ladder of both remote firms sits at or
  below the entry price of the one Peninsula firm found publishing rates.
    remote entry, both firms            $495
    remote mid                          $647 to $1,726
    remote top                        $2,495 to $2,500 discounted, $3,850 list
    local Peninsula firm, entry       $1,500
    local Peninsula firm, top         $3,500
  The two categories converge only at the top of the range. Below roughly
  $1,500 the published market is remote-only.
WHY IT MATTERS: A local firm sending a person to a property cannot meet the
  sub-$1,500 published tier, and the remote firms have no marginal cost of
  travel. The low tier is structurally theirs.
SCOPE COMPARISON: What the remote fee buys is concept-stage work: a layout,
  renders, and a plant and material list, produced without a survey. Against the
  published phase weights recorded earlier in this study, schematic design is 18
  of the 69 points spanned by a design-only engagement, roughly 26 percent. The
  survey and construction-document work that makes a design buildable, the
  remaining 74 percent, is not part of what these firms sell.
CAVEAT: The local comparison rests on a single firm, so "the low tier is
  remote-only" is a statement about published prices, not about what local firms
  actually charge on quotation.
