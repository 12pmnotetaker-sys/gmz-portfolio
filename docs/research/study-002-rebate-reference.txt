WATER REBATE QUICK REFERENCE, GMZ PENINSULA TOWNS
Internal one-page sales reference. All figures below were checked on 2026-08-15.

READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING ANY NUMBER
Rebate amounts change, often at the fiscal year, and every figure on this page carries the date it was checked. This document must never be used to state a rebate as certain. Eligibility, the final amount, and approval are always the water agency's call, never GMZ's. Before a dollar figure goes into any proposal or conversation, re-check the agency page that week.

The one rule that applies everywhere: the client must apply and hold the agency's written go-ahead (a Notice to Proceed) before any lawn is removed. Turf removed early is permanently ineligible. GMZ sequences every job so demolition day comes after the Notice to Proceed arrives.

TOWN BY TOWN

REDWOOD CITY
Rate: $2.00 per sq ft (checked 2026-08-15). Program: Lawn Be Gone!, run through the regional water conservation program for San Mateo County agencies. Cap: not stated on the program page; confirm with the agency. Key condition: submit the application and receive a Notice to Proceed from the water provider before removing any lawn. Apply through: bayareaconservation.org/rebates/lawn. Caution: the program page is undated and its linked documents reference older fiscal years, so confirm the current-year rate with the agency before quoting.

MENLO PARK
Rate: $3.00 per sq ft (checked 2026-08-15). Same program, same Notice to Proceed rule, same application page and same currency caution as Redwood City. Cap not stated on the page.

HILLSBOROUGH
Rate: $1.00 per sq ft (checked 2026-08-15). Same program, same Notice to Proceed rule, same application page and same currency caution. Cap not stated on the page.

PALO ALTO AND LOS ALTOS
Rate: $2.00 per sq ft through the Santa Clara County water district's Landscape Rebate Program (checked 2026-08-15). Cap: $3,000 default for single-family residential; a $6,000 cost-share cap applies in some areas. Key conditions: a completed pre-inspection (the lawn must still be in place) and a written Notice to Proceed before any lawn removal or any material purchase; projects started early are permanently ineligible. Funding is stated to last until June 30, 2027 or until depleted. Apply through: valleywater.org/saving-water/rebates-surveys/landscape-rebates. Note: Palo Alto does not appear on the San Mateo County program's participating-agency list, so the county district program is the confirmed route; also confirm with the client's own water utility whether a separate city program applies.

SAN CARLOS AND BELMONT
Check with your water supplier. The regional program page lists Mid-Peninsula Water District at $4.00 per sq ft (checked 2026-08-15), but whether that district serves a given San Carlos or Belmont address was not confirmed from an agency page. Confirm the client's supplier first; if it is Mid-Peninsula Water District, the $4.00 rate and the Notice to Proceed rule apply through bayareaconservation.org/rebates/lawn.

ATHERTON, WOODSIDE AND PORTOLA VALLEY
Check with your water supplier. No agency page confirmed a town-specific rebate rate for these towns. Many addresses here are served by an investor-owned water utility whose lawn-to-garden program is confirmed to exist, with these confirmed terms (checked 2026-08-15): pre-approval is required before the project begins, at least 3 approved plants per 100 sq ft converted, and artificial turf does not qualify. That utility does not publish its per-square-foot rate on its public page; the rate is visible only inside the customer's own conservation account. The honest move: have the client open their conservation account during the design phase so the real number is on the table before pricing. Program page: calwater.com/conservation-rebates.

EXTRAS
A $300 flat rain-garden rebate is listed on the regional San Mateo County program page where the agency participates (checked 2026-08-15); confirm participation per agency.

CONDITIONS THAT SHOW UP ACROSS PROGRAMS
1. Application approved and written Notice to Proceed in hand before removal. Every confirmed program requires this.
2. Pre-inspection with the lawn still alive and in place (confirmed for the Santa Clara County district program). Do not let a client stop watering to "kill it off early" before approval.
3. Plant coverage minimums (confirmed at 3 approved plants per 100 sq ft for the investor-owned utility). Expect similar elsewhere.
4. Artificial turf does not qualify (confirmed for the investor-owned utility; assume the same elsewhere until an agency page says otherwise).

WHAT TO SAY TO A CLIENT (adapt as needed, keep the hedges)
Most water suppliers in our area will actually pay you to take out a thirsty lawn and replace it with a garden suited to our climate. The amount depends on who supplies your water, and the rules matter: your application has to be approved, and you need the agency's written go-ahead in hand, before a single square foot of lawn comes out. We build that step into the project schedule so nothing gets forfeited. The rebate is always the agency's decision, not ours, so we treat it as a welcome bonus rather than a promise, and we help you confirm the current amount for your address before we ever set a demolition date.

OPERATING NOTES FOR THE GMZ TEAM
Sequence every rebate job: design, application, pre-inspection if required, Notice to Proceed received, then demo. Put the Notice to Proceed date on the schedule as a gate, not a hope. Never write a rebate amount into a proposal as a credit against GMZ's price; it is the agency's money paid on the agency's terms.
