GMZ Landscaping Inc. Proposed operating process Internal — draft for review

Lead to Closeout

A target-state process for design/build and maintenance, built around what GMZ already does well and closing the two ends where the work currently lives in one person's head.

01

The argument

GMZ's estimating is better instrumented than most contractors twice its size. Routing, take-off, level discipline, floor checks, a document set that reconciles to itself. That part is not the problem and nothing here touches it.

The losses are at the two ends. Before a scope exists, there is no qualification step and no shared record, so unqualified site visits burn truck time and real leads go unrecorded. After signature, contract, handover, warranty and renewal all run on judgment, which works while you are the one exercising it and cannot be delegated, published, or compared against by a prospect choosing between three bids.

Everything proposed below is standard practice for residential design/build at this size. None of it is exotic and none of it needs new software to start.

The timing that shapes this

Filoli closed on 10 August. It was the largest account and it held all of Tuesdays. That capacity is open now, and referrals arrive at the pace they arrive at. Online is the only one of the three lead channels that can be turned up deliberately, which is why the website and the qualification step sit at the front of the plan.

02

What does not change

Keep

The estimating engine

One engine, one set of numbers, floors checked before anything reaches a client. It is the strongest asset in the business.

Keep

FreshBooks as the book

Estimates and invoices stay where the accountant can see them. No parallel money system.

Keep

Two separate service lines

Design/build and maintenance have different rates, different agreements and different law behind them. They stay apart.

Keep

The signed design agreement

Design is already a paid product with its own document. That is ahead of most of the trade and it becomes the spine of the new funnel.

03

The design/build funnel

Twelve stages, first contact to follow-up. Seven are new and five are reshaped; nothing in this track stays exactly as it runs today. The order matters: each gate exists so the next stage is only run on work worth running it on.

Stage How it runs now Proposed  
01Inquiry Phone, text, WhatsApp, email, referral. Nothing shared. Every lead lands in one place on arrival, whoever took the call. Name, town, scope, channel, date. New
02Phone qualification None. The site visit is the first filter. Fifteen minutes on the phone: scope, budget band, timeline, who decides, where it is. Decline or defer here, not after the drive. New
03Site consultation Free visit, unstructured. Same visit, run off a checklist so nothing gets missed and the notes come back in a usable shape. Changed
04Concept and planning budget Complimentary concept, then a planning budget with a range. Same two deliverables, sold as a small paid first phase. Cheap enough to be an easy yes, real enough to filter. Changed
05Design development One design agreement covering everything. Second phase of the same agreement, entered only if they approve the concept. Measured survey and drawings. This is the gate that earns a firm price. Changed
06Bid presentation Package sent. Presented in person, plan and price together, decision asked for in the room. Changed
07Construction agreement Varies. Some jobs on an accepted estimate. One compliant contract on every job above $500, with milestone payments and the statutory deposit cap. New
08Pre-construction meeting None. Half an hour before the first truck: access, gate codes, hours, parking, pets, kids, who to call, what the first week looks like. New
09Build Updates when they happen. One update a week, same day: what changed, what is next, what needs a decision, with a photo. Changed
10Substantial completion Varies by job. Walkthrough with a written punch list, every job. Both parties hold a copy. New
11Closeout None. A package handed over: plan copy, plant list, irrigation zone map and controller settings, care guide, the written warranty, and the maintenance proposal. New
12Follow-up None. A call at thirty days and again at six months. Cheapest referral and maintenance conversion available. New

Why phasing the design agreement is the key move

It solves the argument the whole business currently has with clients. Today the ask is "buy a full design before you know what this costs." Phased, the ask is "buy a concept and a budget for a small fee; if you like it, the next step measures and prices it firmly." The first is a leap. The second is a step, and it pays for the design time that is currently given away.

04

The maintenance track

Shorter, and it renews rather than ending. Two changes carry almost all the value.

Stage How it runs now Proposed  
01Inquiry Referral, build handover, online search. Same three channels, recorded in the same place as build leads. Changed
02Route-fit screen None. Check the address against the existing route before quoting. An off-route stop costs far more than it looks like it does. New
03Property walk and quote Walk, then quote on the maintenance book. Unchanged. Keep
04Service agreement Agreement, schedule and its own terms. Same, with renewal date, notice period and price escalation stated on the face of it. Changed
05Onboarding visit First visit is a normal visit. First visit is an assessment: photos, baseline condition, irrigation walked zone by zone. Sets the record for everything after. New
06Route Fixed day, weekly or biweekly. Unchanged. Keep
07Annual review Varies by account. One fixed month a year, every account reviewed: scope still right, price re-struck, agreement renewed. New

Route density is the only lever on maintenance margin

The maintenance book recovers a smaller share of each paid hour than the build book does, and the entire difference is drive time between stops. That is a measured fact, not an estimate. Clustering accounts geographically is the one change that moves it, and it compounds across all twenty-four. A screen at intake costs nothing and stops the problem getting worse.

05

Four things to write once

Most of the proposal above is a habit, not a document. These four are documents, and each gets written once and reused on every job.

One page

Qualification script

The questions asked on every inbound call, in order, with the point at which to decline. Anyone answering the phone can run it.

Needs a lawyer

Construction contract

One compliant template with the required notices, the three-day cancellation right, a milestone payment schedule and the deposit cap.

Half a page

Written warranty

One year on workmanship from completion, plants excluded and the reason stated. Limits exposure while reading as generous.

A template

Closeout package

Plan copy, plant list, irrigation zone map and controller settings, care guide, warranty, maintenance proposal. An hour to assemble per job.

06

Systems

The instinct at this point is to buy a field-service platform. Do not, yet. Configuring software around a process that has not been decided means encoding the current mess at some expense, and the estimating engine already outperforms the quoting module of anything on the market at this price.

Codify the process first. Revisit the platform question in six months, when there is a defined process for it to hold.

Do now

One lead inbox

A single shared place every inquiry gets written down, whatever channel it arrived on. A spreadsheet is enough to start.

Do now

Revive Asana as the pipeline

It is already structured project-per-client and it has been dormant since 2024. Stages matching the funnel above, and one look at it a week.

Do next

Feed the actuals

The workbook has an estimate-versus-actual tab and nothing fills it. Even a monthly manual pass tells you which jobs actually made money.

Later

Revisit a platform

Once the process is fixed and the pipeline has a year of real data behind it, buy the tool that fits it rather than the other way round.

07

Sequence

Ordered by return against effort. A four-person company cannot do all of this at once and does not need to.

First — weeks

Turn on the front door

  • Wire the contact form on the website. Online is already a live channel with nowhere to land.
  • Publish a starting-at figure so prospects self-qualify before they call. The number is yours to set.
  • Write the qualification script and use it on every inbound call.
  • Open one shared lead inbox.
  • Write the one-year workmanship warranty.

Cost: a few hours and one decision. Nothing here needs a lawyer or a purchase.

Second — a month

Fix the paperwork

  • Have a compliant construction contract drawn and use it above $500, every time.
  • Split the design agreement into a concept phase and a design-development phase.
  • Add renewal, notice and escalation terms to the maintenance agreement.

Cost: a lawyer's time. This is the item with the worst downside if left.

Third — a quarter

Close the back end

  • Pre-construction meeting on every job.
  • Weekly client update, same day each week.
  • Written punch list at substantial completion.
  • Closeout package template, then used on every job.
  • Thirty-day and six-month follow-up calls.

Cost: discipline rather than money. Roughly two hours per job, and it is the part that generates referrals.

Fourth — this year

Put the route and the pipeline on a footing

  • Pick the annual review month and run all twenty-four accounts through it.
  • Screen new maintenance leads for route fit before quoting.
  • Revive Asana as the pipeline and start feeding job actuals.

Cost: one concentrated week, then an hour a week.

08

Decisions this needs from you

  1. The starting-at figure

    The smallest design/build project worth mobilizing for. Publishing it is the single highest-leverage qualification tool available, and it costs nothing.

  2. What the concept phase costs

    Small enough to be an easy yes, large enough to filter. It should cover Liliana's time rather than be a token.

  3. Whether the site consultation stays free

    Charging filters hard, and it also suppresses volume. With Tuesdays open, my read is keep it free for now and revisit once the pipeline is full. Your call.

  4. The warranty term

    One year on workmanship is standard and is what I would write. Plants stay excluded, with the reason stated, because you do not control the watering.

  5. The annual review month for maintenance

    Pick a quiet one. It becomes the moment a price change can actually reach twenty-four accounts.

  6. Who owns the lead inbox

    Whoever it is has to look at it daily. This is the one item that fails quietly if nobody is named.
