Lead intake, client side and ours

Two forms. The client fills the first, in their own words. Whoever takes the call fills the second, and by the end of it the lead is either a booked visit or a polite no. Both are built so the answers land where the estimate later needs them.

WHY

Four rules the forms are built on

Never ask a client to prescribe the work

Field research on service trades found that a customer who volunteers a confident but wrong self-diagnosis gets quoted more, not less, and a correct one buys them nothing. So the client form asks what is wrong and what they want out of the space. It never asks what should be installed. That is what the paid design step is for.

Budget is the qualification gate, and it is asked early

A band, not a number, with "not sure yet" allowed. It is the one field that decides whether a truck leaves the yard, and asking it on the form is kinder than discovering the mismatch after the drive.

The service line is decided once, at intake

Design build or maintenance, chosen before anything is priced, because the two lines carry different rates and different agreements. A lead that reads as both is two leads. The staff form makes that call explicit and records why.

Three gates that cost real money if missed

A client aged 65 or over gets five days to cancel rather than three. New landscape over roughly 500 square feet with a permit pulls in the state water documentation package and a third-party irrigation audit nobody at GMZ can sign. And on any rebate job, turf removed before the water agency's written go-ahead is permanently ineligible. All three sit on the staff form as hard stops.

What is still yours to set

  • The budget bands above are placeholders. They should bracket your real work, and the lowest band should sit where a build crew stops being the right answer.
  • Whether the client form is a web form, a paper sheet, or both. It is written to work as either.
  • Who owns the lead record and looks at it daily. This is the one item that fails quietly if nobody is named.
  • The hours-and-miles field is deliberate. Ninety days of it answers whether phone qualification pays, which no published research can tell you.
