What the client came for

When are you coming, and did anything happen. Both are answered above the fold, in that order, because that is the order they are asked in.

The one thing that moves

Most weeks the middle card is absent and Today is two cards long. That is the design working: a portal that always has something demanding attention trains a client to ignore it. When the amber card does appear, it is the only amber on the screen.

Language sits in the chrome

The switch is next to the account name rather than buried in a settings screen, because a client who needs Spanish needs it on the first screen, not after finding a menu.

The crew writes in Spanish

This is the sentence the whole portal depends on, and asking a crew lead to compose it in English would mean either nothing gets written or something worse than nothing does. Rarely opened, and that is fine

Nobody reads seventy visits for pleasure. The record earns its place at three moments: a dispute about whether something was done, a house going on the market, and a new gardener or owner taking over. All three are worth the storage.

On a laptop this is the whole page

The dense two-column layout from the service record concept is what this becomes at width: the account rail on the left, dated entries and the irrigation grid on the right. The phone gets the same data as a list.

It prints

A year of the record on paper is what a client hands to whoever comes next. That is a real use, so the wide layout keeps a print stylesheet.

Structured, not a chat box

A free-text message translated across languages loses exactly the part that matters: what and where. Three fields plus a photo survive translation, and they arrive as something the office can act on rather than something it has to interpret.

The original is never thrown away

Every message stores what the person wrote and the translation shown to the other side. The chip says which language it came from, and one tap shows it. Nobody is ever quoted words they did not write, in either direction.

A request is not an approval

The composer says so before the send button, in the client's own language. It is the same promise the site already makes, moved to the moment where it actually gets tested.

The phone stays the urgent path

A water leak is a phone call. Putting "call instead" next to "send it" is honest about what a message queue can and cannot do, and it keeps the portal from becoming the place urgent things go to sit.

What happens to a request

The rules

How the two languages work

Each side writes in their own language

The client picks English or Spanish for the whole portal. The crew and the office write in theirs. Nobody composes in a second language, because that is where meaning goes missing and where the writing stops happening at all.

The original is stored, always

Both the original and the translation are kept on every message and every request. The provenance chip names the language it was written in, and the original is one tap away, so a translation never passes itself off as somebody's own words.

Structure beats free text

What, where and a photo. Three short fields translate cleanly and arrive actionable, where a paragraph arrives as a puzzle. The place names come from the client's own garden rather than a text box.

Numbers, dates and money are not translated prose

A service day, a quote reference and a date render from the account data in the reader's language and format. They are never passed through a translation, because that is the one place a mistake is expensive.

Nothing in the portal promises a reply time

No response-time commitment appears anywhere, because none has been decided. The same rule as an unpublished answer on the site: a client will hold GMZ to whatever it said. When a commitment exists, it belongs here and in the FAQ at the same time.

Urgent stays a phone call

The composer offers the phone next to the send button. A message queue is the wrong channel for water running down a driveway, and the design says so rather than hoping.

Prototype notes

  • This supersedes the three separate concepts as the direction. They are kept in this folder as the record of what was considered, and each now lives on as a depth of this one product.
  • Spanish text is marked with lang="es" so a screen reader switches voice rather than reading Spanish with an English one. The same applies to the language switch.
  • Spanish in the samples is written as GMZ speaks it, not machine output. Real strings need a bilingual pass before launch: a translated portal with awkward Spanish reads worse to a Spanish-speaking client than an English one.
  • No response-time promise, no lead-time promise and no fee appears anywhere. Nothing here states a term that has not been decided.
  • No dollar figures. Approving a quote refers to the quote, which is where the price lives.
  • The tree badge is a stand-in until the real GMZ mark exists as SVG. Photo slots are labelled plates.
  • Accessibility kept: skip link, visible focus rings, aria-current on the active tab in every screen, one h1, alt text on every plate, diagrams carrying their claim as an aria-label, wide figures scrolling inside their own container, and a reduced-motion block.
  • Client name, dates, crew notes and message text are invented sample data.
