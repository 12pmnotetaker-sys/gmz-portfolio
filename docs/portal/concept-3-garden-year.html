What your agreement says

  • Month to month. You are not locked in.
  • Either side can end it with 30 days' notice in writing. No penalty.
  • One set price per visit, agreed before the first visit.
  • A change of cadence changes the agreement, confirmed in writing.
  • The price is reviewed once a year, printed on the face of the agreement.
  • Most work goes ahead in light rain. When conditions would make it damaging we move the visit inside the same week and tell you.

Prototype notes

  • Concept 3 of 3. The organising idea is the year: the client sees the season coming, which is also what makes weekly cadence and seasonal extra work make sense rather than feel like an upsell. Dark first by choice, with the light palette defined in full.
  • The year ribbon is a categorical timeline. Its three hues were checked with a colour-vision-deficiency validator against both the dark and light surfaces, every segment carries its own text label, and a table view of the same data sits under it, so nothing is carried by colour alone.
  • Work that is quoted separately is drawn as an outline rather than a fill, because it is not part of the account until the client approves it.
  • The tree badge is a stand-in until the real GMZ mark exists as SVG.
  • Photo slots are labelled plates, not photographs.
  • No dollar figures. Every policy sentence traces to an FAQ already marked published, so nothing here states a term that has not been decided.
  • Accessibility kept: skip link, visible focus rings, aria-current on the active nav item, one h1, alt text on every plate, tabular figures, wide content scrolling in its own container, and a reduced-motion block.
  • Client name, dates and notes are invented sample data.
