What actually happens

From the first call to a number you can plan against.

  1. A short call

    What you want done, where the property is, and what you are planning to spend. You will not get a price on this call.

  2. A walk of the property

    Access, grade, drainage and what is already growing there. A working conversation rather than a sales call.

  3. A concept and a planning range

    When it makes sense, a short first design phase. It is paid, and you see the fee before it starts.

Why no price on the call? Landscape work is priced off a measured site and chosen materials. Anyone pricing your yard over the phone is guessing at both.

The awkward questions

The four things people ask us first

Every one of these is answered in full on the site, in plain language, before you are asked to commit to anything.

Why it is a range and not a price

A landscape price is built from a measured site and chosen materials. Until both exist, an honest number is a range, and a firm-looking number is a guess wearing a suit. The range narrows as the work gets defined, and the measuring costs you nothing.

Read the answer

Why design is paid, and what it does not do

Design is its own step with its own agreement, priced as a share of the construction cost. It is not folded into the build and it is not credited against it. What you are buying is the plan itself.

Read the answer

Changes, and what is under the ground

Almost every dispute on a landscape job starts with something nobody wrote down. The defense is not a padded price. It is a named exclusion, a written change order before any extra work starts, and being told what was found the day it is found.

Read the answer

How long it takes, and how long your yard is a site

Two different questions. One is how far out the schedule runs, the other is how many weeks there is a crew in your garden. A new garden is not finished on the last day: it is finished a season later.
