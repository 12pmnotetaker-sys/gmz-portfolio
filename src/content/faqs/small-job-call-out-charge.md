Yes. A one-off visit carries a minimum, and you hear what it is before anyone comes out.

A small job is its own kind of work rather than a small version of a project, and pricing it that way is what keeps it fair. Pruning a few shrubs, replacing a valve or tidying one bed is a round trip for a short piece of work, and the drive is the same length whether the job takes twenty minutes or most of the morning. So a service call carries a minimum that covers getting someone to you and the first stretch of work, with time beyond that added on. You are told the minimum on the call and it is paid before the visit, so nothing has to be decided on your doorstep.

Not every small job is the same size of visit, and there are two. Most are one person for a couple of hours: a sprinkler that will not come on, a controller nobody can program, a valve, a few shrubs. Some genuinely need two people and a truck, because the work involves weight, a machine or hauling something away, and those carry a higher minimum. You are told on the call which one your job is and why, before anyone is booked.

Two things make a real difference to what you get for it. If several small items have been piling up, put them all on one visit: one trip covering five things is far better value than five trips covering one each. And if what you actually want is recurring rather than one-off, say so on the call, because regular upkeep runs under a maintenance agreement at a set price per visit, and for the same work repeated it is usually the cheaper way to buy it.

One distinction worth being clear about, because the two get confused and they are priced differently. A site consultation is someone coming to look at your property and talk through what you want, with nothing done to the garden: you are buying an hour and an opinion. A service call is work: someone arrives with tools and leaves the job finished. If what you want is the thing fixed rather than assessed, ask for the service call, because paying for a consultation and then paying again to do the work is the expensive way round.
