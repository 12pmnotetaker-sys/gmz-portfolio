Water, delivered the right way, matters more than everything else combined in the first year. New plants are growing roots, not showing off. Water deeply and less often so the moisture goes down and the roots follow it; short daily sprinkles keep roots at the surface and make a plant that cannot survive its first hot week. If something looks stressed between cycles, hand water that plant rather than raising the schedule for the whole garden.

Two other things carry most of the rest. Keep mulch topped up and pulled back off the stems, and pull weeds while they are small, because freshly worked soil grows them fast. Do not push fertilizer in the first season and do not prune new shrubs hard: both work against a plant that is trying to root. Expect some settling in new beds and paths, and expect a few plants to sit and sulk for a season before they move.

By the second summer the garden can run on a normal schedule. The first year is the one that decides whether it gets there, which is also why we always offer to maintain it ourselves.
