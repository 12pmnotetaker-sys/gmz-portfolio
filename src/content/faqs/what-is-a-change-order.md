A change order is a written amendment to the contract, priced and signed by both of us before the work happens, covering anything that was not in the original scope. Nothing is added to your bill without one. If anyone on a job site tells you a change is no problem and it never appears in writing, that is not generosity; it is a bill you have not seen yet.

There are three causes and two of them are avoidable. Scope you add mid-job, which is fine and is what change orders are for: you see the wall going up and want it longer. Decisions made late, a surface chosen after the base is already in. And genuine unknowns underground, which is the third and is the reason the exclusions page exists at all.

How to keep the count near zero: choose your materials before we start rather than during, read the exclusions page and ask about anything you assumed was included, let us assess the drainage instead of assuming the existing setup is fine, and make your list of while-you're-here additions before the contract rather than in week two. Adding something at bid time is nearly always cheaper than adding it after the crew has moved past that part of the yard and has to come back to it.
