California caps the deposit on a home improvement contract at $1,000 or 10 percent of the contract price, whichever is less, and that is what we take. It is the law, and a contractor asking for more up front is telling you something.

After that, payments follow the work rather than the calendar. Your contract lists a payment for each completed phase: typically site preparation and grading, then hardscape, then irrigation, then planting. Each payment comes due when its phase is done and you can see it standing in your yard. The final payment waits for the walkthrough and the cleared punch list.

The exact schedule is written into your contract for your project. The shape is always the same: you pay for work you can see, and completion is worth holding money for.
