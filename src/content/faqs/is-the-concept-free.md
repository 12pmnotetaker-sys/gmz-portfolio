No, and neither is the visit that comes before it. They are two separate things and each carries its own price, which is worth understanding because they buy you different things.

The visit is an hour on your property with someone who knows the trade: they walk it with you, look at access, grade and drainage, and tell you straight what they think is possible and what it is likely to involve. It carries a fixed fee you know before you book. Nothing is produced on paper and nothing commits you to anything.

What comes after it is the first phase of the design agreement: a written concept and the planning budget that goes with it, a considered look at what could happen on your property and a range to plan around, put on paper by somebody who has stood on the site. It is a short phase, priced on its own, and you can stop there. You see the fee in writing before you agree to it.

We do this way on purpose. A concept given away costs the person receiving it nothing, so it tends to be treated as worth nothing, and the temptation is to produce it quickly rather than properly. Paying for it means the time behind it is real and the number attached to it can be defended. The same logic reaches back to the visit: an hour of genuine attention on your property is worth something, and pricing it means you get that hour rather than a sales call built around getting to the next step.

What the concept still is not is a bid. A price you can commit to comes after a measured plan and chosen materials.
